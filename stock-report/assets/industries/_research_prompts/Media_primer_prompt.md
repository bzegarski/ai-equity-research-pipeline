## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the MEDIA industry — broadcasting, cable networks, advertising agencies, publishing — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the canonical Buffett media writeups (the Washington Post 1973 purchase, Buffalo News 1977, Capital Cities/ABC), the structural collapse of advertising-supported media businesses over the past two decades as audiences fragmented to streaming and digital platforms, the cord-cutting impact on cable networks, and the structurally healthy sub-segments (out-of-home advertising, certain niche publishers) that survived the secular shift.

What to attend to particularly for Media. The industry's defining feature over the past two decades has been secular decline of advertising-supported traditional media against the rise of streaming and digital platforms. The Washington Post (Buffett 1973 purchase, multi-decade hold, 2014 sale of newspapers to Jeff Bezos personally) is the canonical wisdom-tradition writeup of a media business — Buffett's commentary in the 1985, 1991, 2007, and 2013 letters traces the rise and fall of the local-newspaper monopoly business model. The Buffalo News (1977 acquisition; 2020 sale) is the parallel cautionary lesson on small-newspaper economics. (1) Cable networks and broadcasters — Disney (post-2018 21st Century Fox acquisition; major theme parks plus ESPN plus Hulu plus Disney+), Warner Bros. Discovery (post-2022 Discovery / WarnerMedia merger; CNN, HBO, Discovery, Warner Bros. studios), Paramount Global (CBS, Showtime, Paramount+), Fox Corporation (Fox News, Fox Sports), Comcast's NBCUniversal (also classified under Cable). All have struggled with cord-cutting trajectories and the high cost of streaming-platform investment. (2) Publishing and content — The New York Times (the canonical structural-survivor digital-subscription case study; ~10M digital subscribers as of 2024–25; one of the few traditional publishers that successfully pivoted), News Corp (Wall Street Journal, Dow Jones, HarperCollins, Australian and UK publishing), The Atlantic (private), Hearst (private). (3) Advertising agencies and ad-tech — Omnicom Group, Interpublic, WPP (UK-listed), Publicis (Paris-listed), Dentsu (Japan-listed). The agency holding-company business has been pressured by direct-to-consumer and platform-direct ad buying for a decade. (4) Out-of-home advertising — Lamar Advertising, Outfront Media, Clear Channel Outdoor. Structurally healthier than print or broadcast advertising; cord-cutting doesn't affect billboards. (5) Niche and trade publishers — IHS Markit (acquired by S&P Global), Wolters Kluwer, RELX, Daily Journal Corp (Munger's company; legal information services). The wisdom-tradition references for this industry are deep — Buffett's Washington Post (1973 purchase commentary, multi-decade Berkshire-letter discussion), Buffalo News (1977 acquisition discussion), Capital Cities/ABC (the partnership with Tom Murphy as the canonical "outsider CEO" case), Munger's Daily Journal (long-running commentary on legal-information-services and on broader media). The structural framework: traditional advertising-supported media business models compress when audience fragments and ad rates fall; the media businesses that earned multi-decade compounding (Washington Post 1970s–90s, Capital Cities 1985 broadcast-cable peak) typically did so during periods of audience scarcity and ad-rate strength that no longer exist.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's Washington Post (1973 onward) and Buffalo News (1977 onward) commentary across Berkshire letters. Munger's Daily Journal annual meeting transcripts. The Pew Research Journalism reports for free media-industry data. eMarketer / Insider Intelligence for ad-spend and cord-cutting data. Damodaran's media-sector data. Stratechery on platform-vs-pipe dynamics in media (the Disney / Netflix / streaming wars commentary specifically). Specific industry trade press — Variety, The Hollywood Reporter, Digiday.

Length: 4,000–5,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Media businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
