## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the BUILDING PRODUCTS industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for how building-products companies segment by end market (new residential, repair-and-remodel, non-residential), how their distribution channels (homebuilders, big-box retail, professional dealers) shape unit economics, and which sub-segments inside the industry have durable brand/category moats vs which are pure cyclicals.

What to attend to particularly for Building Products. The industry's defining feature is the split across three end markets that move on different cycles. New residential construction (housing starts) drives roughly 30–40% of demand and is the most cyclical piece — peak-to-trough variations of 60% over the 2006–2009 cycle. Repair-and-remodel (R&R) is the larger and more stable piece — homes need roofs, water heaters, HVAC, plumbing, and windows replaced regardless of housing-start cycles, producing a steadier ~50% of industry demand. Non-residential construction is the smallest and longest-cycle piece. The canonical names span product categories: Masco (faucets, paints), Fortune Brands Innovations (locks, plumbing), Allegion (locks), Owens Corning (insulation, roofing), Carrier (HVAC), Lennox (HVAC), Trane Technologies (HVAC), Sherwin-Williams (paint, also Materials), AAON, Watsco (HVAC distribution), Pool Corporation (pool products distribution), Mohawk Industries (flooring), Whirlpool (appliances). Distribution channel is the moat axis to attend to — branded plumbing fixtures and locks have channel positions through homebuilders and Big-Box retail (Home Depot, Lowe's) that newer entrants find hard to displace. HVAC has the additional dimension of dealer-installer training and refrigerant transitions (the 2024–25 phase-down to A2L refrigerants is the current cycle event). Pat Dorsey's Little Book on intangible-asset moats and switching-cost moats applies to brand-driven categories. Buffett's Berkshire owns Benjamin Moore, Acme Brick, MiTek, Marmon's building-product holdings — and the broader Berkshire commentary on durable-brand businesses applies. The single most useful indicator for cycle context is U.S. existing-home sales (R&R is correlated with home turnover) plus the NAHB Housing Market Index for forward demand sentiment.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The U.S. Census Bureau Construction Spending and Housing Starts data (free monthly). The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) Housing Market Index. The Joint Center for Housing Studies (Harvard) Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity (LIRA) for R&R cycle data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book chapter on brand and switching-cost moats. Damodaran's building-products data.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Building Products businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
