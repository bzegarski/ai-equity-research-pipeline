## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the GROUND TRANSPORTATION industry — North American Class I railroads and trucking — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for why North American Class I railroads have one of the deepest physical-asset moats in U.S. equities (right-of-way, terminal access, decades of construction), how the rail oligopoly differs from trucking economics, what precision scheduled railroading (PSR) did to the industry's profitability, and why Buffett's BNSF acquisition (2009) is the canonical wisdom-tradition rail writeup.

What to attend to particularly for Ground Transportation. The industry has two structurally different halves. (1) North American Class I railroads — Union Pacific, Norfolk Southern, CSX, Canadian National, Canadian Pacific Kansas City, plus BNSF (private, owned by Berkshire Hathaway since 2009). Six operators control U.S. and Canadian Class I rail, and the right-of-way moat is one of the most genuinely durable in U.S. equities — building a new interstate rail line is essentially impossible for permitting, eminent-domain, and capital-cost reasons. Buffett's BNSF acquisition (announced November 2009 for $34B + assumption of debt; the 2009 letter on the deal and subsequent letters across 2010–2024 are the canonical wisdom-tradition rail writings) is the deepest treatment available. Precision Scheduled Railroading (PSR), pioneered by Hunter Harrison at CN, then CSX, then NS — moving from a hub-and-spoke yard system to point-to-point scheduled service — improved operating ratios from the high 70s to the mid 60s industry-wide between 2014 and 2024 but at significant operational and customer-service cost. The trade-off between operating-ratio improvement and revenue-mix degradation is a central management question. (2) Trucking — divides into Truckload (TL: Knight-Swift, Werner, Schneider, J.B. Hunt's TL piece) and Less-than-Truckload (LTL: Old Dominion, Saia, XPO Logistics' LTL, ArcBest, Estes — Yellow Corporation collapsed in 2023). Trucking is fragmented (the largest TL carrier holds <5% of the market), capital-light per shipment but capex-heavy at the company level, and deeply cyclical with manufacturing and retail volumes. LTL is more consolidated than TL because of terminal-density requirements (a national LTL network needs 200+ terminals; the cost of building one from scratch is prohibitive). Old Dominion Freight Line is the canonical operating excellence case in LTL, with 20%+ operating margin against industry-median 5–10%. Driver labor markets, fuel costs, and the spot rate vs contract rate spread are the principal cycle drivers. The Driver Shortage / Driver Surplus cycle is shorter than railroad cycles and produces dramatic spot-rate swings. Wisdom-tradition writings here are concentrated in Buffett's BNSF commentary and Charlie Munger's broader thinking on infrastructure-moat businesses.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's BNSF commentary across Berkshire letters (2009 onward — cite each year). The Association of American Railroads (AAR) for weekly carload data and operating-ratio benchmarks. The American Trucking Associations (ATA) for trucking-industry data. The Surface Transportation Board for rail regulatory matters. The Cass Freight Index for U.S. freight volume. Damodaran's transportation data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on physical-asset and infrastructure moats. Specific industry trade press — Trains magazine, FreightWaves, Journal of Commerce.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Ground Transportation businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
