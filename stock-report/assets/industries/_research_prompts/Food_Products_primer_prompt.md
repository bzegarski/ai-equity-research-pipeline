## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the FOOD PRODUCTS industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the secular pressure of private-label growth on packaged-food brand premiums, the structural difference between commodity packaged foods and premium / specialty foods, the unique economics of agricultural commodity processors (ADM, Bunge, Tyson), and Buffett's Kraft Heinz cautionary lesson on brand-decay and cost-cutting limits.

What to attend to particularly for Food Products. The industry breaks into three structurally different sub-segments. (1) Branded packaged foods — Mondelez, Hershey, General Mills, Kellogg, Kraft Heinz, J.M. Smucker, Conagra, Campbell Soup, McCormick, Lamb Weston (frozen potato; the 2016 Conagra spin), Hormel, Tyson Foods. The structural pressure of the last decade has been private-label growth — store brands (Costco's Kirkland Signature, Trader Joe's, Aldi own-label, Walmart's Great Value) have eroded the brand premium for marginal-utility products. Categories that resist private-label include premium spirits, infant formula, prescription-grade OTC, and a small number of strongly differentiated branded categories (Hershey's chocolate, McCormick's spice). Commodity packaged foods (cold cereal, soup, pasta sauce) have lost steadily. Buffett's Kraft Heinz episode (Berkshire's 2015 partnership with 3G to merge Kraft and Heinz, the 2019 $15B impairment write-down, Buffett's public admission that "we overpaid") is the canonical cautionary lesson on brand decay and the limits of cost-cutting as a strategy. (2) Agricultural commodity processors — Archer Daniels Midland (ADM), Bunge, Tyson Foods (meat), Pilgrim's Pride, JBS USA. Commodity-priced output, vertically integrated supply chains (grain elevators, processing plants, transportation), exposure to crop yields and feed-cost cycles. ADM is the canonical case; the wisdom-tradition writings on ADM are limited but instructive. (3) Specialty / premium foods — McCormick (spices, with strong category leadership), Lamb Weston (frozen potato; B2B foodservice supplier), Cal-Maine Foods (eggs; commodity but with consolidation), Vital Farms (premium eggs), Hain Celestial (organic / natural). The wisdom-tradition references span Buffett's H.J. Heinz commentary (2013 Berkshire / 3G acquisition), the Kraft Heinz cautionary commentary, See's Candies (technically Discretionary but the brand-moat lessons apply), and the broader 2007 Berkshire letter on durable consumer franchises. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on consumer-brand moats applies. The structural threat trajectory worth understanding: private label has gone from <15% share in U.S. packaged food in 2000 to ~20% by 2024, with material acceleration in 2021–24.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's Kraft Heinz commentary (2015 deal letter, 2019 write-down letter, and subsequent annual remarks). Buffett's See's Candies commentary as the brand-moat reference. The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) data for agricultural commodity context. NielsenIQ / Circana scanner data where freely available. The Private Label Manufacturers Association (PLMA) for private-label-share data. Damodaran's food-products data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on brand moats in CPG.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Food Products businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
