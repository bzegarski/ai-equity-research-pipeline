## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the DIVERSIFIED REITs industry — REITs holding multiple property-type portfolios — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the REIT structural framework (90%+ taxable-income distribution requirement; FFO and AFFO as the canonical earnings metrics; cap rate as the canonical valuation language), why diversified REITs typically trade at a discount to specialized property-type peers, and the strategic question of conglomerate-vs-focused real estate that has driven most diversified REITs to either spin or sell down to focused specialty.

What to attend to particularly for Diversified REITs. The industry has shrunk materially over the past decade as the consensus has shifted toward focused property-type REITs (specialized REITs typically trade at higher multiples than diversified). The canonical names are smaller and fewer: WP Carey (formerly W.P. Carey & Co.; net-lease focused, recently de-classified some industrial), VICI Properties (gaming-and-entertainment net-lease; classified Specialized in some screens), Spirit Realty Capital (acquired by Realty Income 2024), STORE Capital (acquired private 2022 by GIC and Oak Street). True diversified REITs (multiple distinct property types managed under one platform) are increasingly rare. The economic engine — collect rent from a portfolio of properties under triple-net or modified-gross leases; pass through to shareholders via the 90%+ distribution requirement; grow through acquisition-and-development; compound book value via property appreciation plus retained-earnings (the small piece that's allowed). The metrics — FFO (Funds From Operations = net income + depreciation + amortization − gains on property sales) is the canonical earnings substitute for REITs because GAAP depreciation on real property is largely fictional; AFFO (Adjusted FFO) further nets out maintenance capex; cap rate (NOI / property value) is the canonical valuation language. The cycle exposure varies by property type within the diversified portfolio, but the holding-company structure produces aggregate cash-flow profiles that reflect the weighted mix. Wisdom-tradition references for REITs in general are sparse — NAREIT's REIT 101 series is the canonical primer; Howard Marks's commentary on cycles in long-duration cash-flow businesses applies; Pat Dorsey on real-estate moats applies. The single most useful frame: diversified REITs trade at a structural discount to focused property-type peers; the analytical move is to ask whether the diversification produces operational benefits (capital-allocation flexibility, defensiveness through cycles) that justify the discount, or whether the holding-company structure produces inferior outcomes vs splitting into focused entities.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. NAREIT (reit.com) REIT 101 series for the canonical REIT primer (FFO, AFFO, cap rate from scratch). Damodaran's real-estate sector data. Howard Marks on long-duration cash-flow cycle businesses. Pat Dorsey on real-estate moats. Specific industry trade press — REITNews, Nareit reports, S&P Global Real Estate.

Length: 3,000–4,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Diversified REITs businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
