## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the COMMERCIAL SERVICES & SUPPLIES industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the route-and-rolling-stock economics that define this industry, the density advantages that produce surprisingly durable moats in waste collection and pest control and uniform rental, the recurring-revenue contract structure, and why this is one of the most under-appreciated corners of compounding businesses in U.S. equity markets.

What to attend to particularly for Commercial Services & Supplies. The single best frame is route-density economics. Waste Management, Republic Services, Stericycle (medical waste), Casella Waste Systems — these companies own collection routes in geographies, and the density of customers per route hour drives operating margin. A waste hauler with 200 customer stops per route in a tight urban geography has 20–40% higher EBITDA margin than one with 80 stops on dispersed rural routes. The route is the moat: a competitor entering would need to win each customer one by one, building density from zero, while the incumbent already has a contracted book and amortized fixed costs. Cintas (uniform rental and facility services), UniFirst, Vestis (the Aramark spin) operate on identical economics — customer pickups and deliveries on weekly routes, with density per route the cost-curve driver. Rollins (the parent of Orkin pest control), Terminix Global (now part of Rentokil), Service Corporation International (funeral services with route-and-density logic at the regional cluster level) all share the structural pattern. Recurring-revenue contracts (typically 1–5 years, auto-renewing) produce 90%+ revenue retention and let these companies compound free cash flow at high rates. The wisdom-tradition writings on this industry are sparse; Buffett's commentary on Marmon's industrial-services holdings and on the broader Berkshire approach to "good business" simple-economics names applies. Pat Dorsey's Little Book chapter on switching-cost and density moats is directly applicable. Capital intensity is moderate — trucks and equipment depreciate over 5–10 years, but landfill ownership in waste is a regulated barrier-to-entry asset that rarely gets fully reflected in book value. Capital allocation through tuck-in acquisitions of regional route operators is the dominant growth engine for the larger names; Republic Services and Waste Management together completed dozens of route acquisitions per year through the 2010–2024 period.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The National Waste & Recycling Association (NWRA) for U.S. waste-industry data. The Textile Rental Services Association for uniform-rental industry data. The National Pest Management Association. Damodaran's commercial-services data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on density and switching-cost moats. Buffett's Marmon and broader Berkshire commentary on simple-economics businesses. Specific industry trade press — Waste Today, Industrial Hygiene Today, Pest Control Technology.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Commercial Services & Supplies businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
