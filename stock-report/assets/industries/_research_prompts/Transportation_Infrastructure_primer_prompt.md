## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the TRANSPORTATION INFRASTRUCTURE industry — toll roads, ports, airports, regional railroads — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the concession-and-toll-road economics that define this industry, why long-duration regulated returns make these businesses bond-proxy-like in market behavior, the difference between concession-rights structures across geographies, and how the privatization wave of the last twenty years has reshaped the listed universe.

What to attend to particularly for Transportation Infrastructure. The U.S. listed universe is small; the global infrastructure private-equity universe is large. The economics are concession-and-toll-road shaped: a private operator obtains a long-term (typically 25–99 year) concession from a government to operate an asset (a toll road, a port terminal, an airport, a bridge, a regional railroad), pays the government an upfront concession fee plus ongoing royalties, and earns the difference between toll/fee revenue and operating cost. The structure produces utility-like cash-flow profiles with very long-duration assets. (1) Listed U.S. names are limited — Genesee & Wyoming was a leading short-line railroad operator until its 2019 take-private; the U.S. has virtually no listed pure-play toll-road operator. (2) Listed European, Australian, Latin American names — Atlantia (Italy; the historical Autostrade), Vinci (France; toll roads plus construction), Eiffage, Ferrovial (Spain; airports plus toll roads), ASTM, Transurban (Australia; toll roads), Auckland International Airport, Aena (Spanish airports). (3) Listed port and terminal operators — DP World (Dubai), Hutchison Ports, COSCO, China Merchants Port. (4) Listed airport operators — Aéroports de Paris (Groupe ADP), Fraport (Germany), Mexico's GAP / OMA / ASUR, Aena (Spain), Sydney Airport (took private). The 2008–09 recession and the 2020 pandemic each produced significant traffic declines and tested concession debt loads — the canonical recent stress tests. Long-cycle interest-rate sensitivity is the dominant macro variable: when long rates rise, concession-cash-flow valuations re-rate downward as bond-proxies. Inflation-linkage is built into many concession structures (toll escalators tied to CPI), which is the differentiating feature from unhedged regulated utilities. Wisdom-tradition writers have engaged this industry sparingly; Howard Marks's writing on cycles in long-duration cash-flow businesses applies. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on regulatory-and-physical-asset moats applies directly. The U.S. opportunity is dominated by private infrastructure funds (Macquarie, KKR Infrastructure, Brookfield Infrastructure Partners) rather than listed pure-plays.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The OECD's Compendium of International Investment in Infrastructure for primer-level industry data. The International Air Transport Association (IATA) for airport-industry data. The International Association of Ports and Harbors. The International Bridge, Tunnel and Turnpike Association for U.S. toll-road data. Damodaran's infrastructure-sector data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on regulatory and physical-asset moats. Howard Marks on long-duration cycle businesses. Brookfield Infrastructure Partners' annual letters as canonical practitioner writing on listed infrastructure.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Transportation Infrastructure businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
