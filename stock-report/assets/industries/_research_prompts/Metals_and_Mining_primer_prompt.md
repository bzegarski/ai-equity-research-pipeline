## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the METALS & MINING industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for why each metal needs to be analyzed separately, why cost-curve quartile position is more important than growth, why capex super-cycles (decade-long) define the industry's returns over time, and why Munger and Buffett are persistently skeptical of mining moats.

What to attend to particularly for Metals & Mining. Each metal has different demand drivers and different supply economics, and a single primer voice should treat them as separate industries. (1) Gold — Newmont, Barrick, Agnico Eagle, Kinross — is a monetary asset with central-bank flows, jewelry demand (mostly India, China, Middle East), and investment demand (ETFs). Gold-mining stocks underperform gold itself over long periods because operating leverage works in both directions and management dilutes equity at cycle peaks. (2) Copper — Freeport-McMoRan, Southern Copper, Antofagasta, Teck — is the electrification metal, structurally short on supply through the 2030s as new mines take 10–15 years to permit and build. (3) Iron ore — Rio Tinto, BHP, Vale, Fortescue — is the Chinese steel cycle in proxy form; the four major producers account for ~70% of global seaborne supply, an unusually consolidated commodity industry. (4) Aluminum — Alcoa, Century Aluminum, Norsk Hydro, Rusal, Chalco — is the most energy-intensive metal (smelter electricity costs are 30–40% of the cost stack), so location near cheap and stable electricity is the structural advantage. (5) Battery and clean-tech metals — lithium (Albemarle, SQM, Pilbara), nickel (Vale, Norilsk, Glencore), cobalt — have demand profiles tied to EV battery production, and their supply economics differ enough that each needs its own paragraph. Reserves accounting matters and is governed by industry codes (SEC's S-K 1300, JORC, NI 43-101). Cost-curve quartile position matters more than growth — the lowest-cost producers (top quartile) survive every cycle; the highest-cost producers (fourth quartile) are the cyclical bankruptcies. Capex super-cycles are decade-long — the 2003–2014 China-led mining capex wave produced overcapacity that took until 2020 to digest; the 2024-onward energy-transition capex cycle is the present version. Munger's persistent skepticism of commodity moats is the wisdom-tradition warning here; Howard Marks's commodity-cycle thinking applies in full.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (free annual reference). The World Gold Council, the International Copper Study Group, the World Steel Association, the International Aluminium Institute. World Bank Commodity Markets Outlook (free quarterly). Howard Marks on commodity cycles. Damodaran's mining and metals data. Specific industry trade press — Mining Journal, S&P Global Commodity Insights summaries where freely available. Goehring & Rozencwajg quarterly market commentary for thoughtful contemporary writing.

Length: 3,500–5,500 words (this industry earns extra length because each major metal needs separate treatment). Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Metals & Mining businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
