## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the AIR FREIGHT & LOGISTICS industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the network-density economics that define logistics, the difference between asset-heavy parcel networks (UPS, FedEx) and asset-light freight forwarders (Expeditors, C.H. Robinson, XPO), the role of less-than-truckload (LTL) economics, and why e-commerce has reshaped the entire industry over the last decade.

What to attend to particularly for Air Freight & Logistics. The industry's defining feature is network-density economics. (1) Parcel networks — UPS, FedEx, DHL — are the classic asset-heavy logistics model: hub-and-spoke routing through a small number of major airfreight hubs (Memphis for FedEx, Louisville for UPS), with daily flight schedules and delivery vehicles fanning out from regional sort centers. The moat is the network: replicating UPS's roughly 500,000 daily delivery routes plus its hub infrastructure would require capital expenditure of tens of billions of dollars over a decade. Density per route (stops per driver per day) is the cost-curve driver, and Amazon's in-housing of last-mile delivery from 2020 onward has been the most significant structural disruptor — Amazon now delivers 70%+ of its own orders, removing the highest-density piece from FedEx and UPS networks. (2) Less-than-truckload (LTL) — Old Dominion Freight Line, Saia, ArcBest, XPO, Estes, Yellow (now bankrupt; the 2023 collapse is the canonical cautionary case). LTL economics depend on terminal density and trailer-fill rates; the strongest operator (Old Dominion) earns operating margins 2–3× the industry median by being disciplined about pricing. (3) Asset-light freight forwarders and brokers — Expeditors International, C.H. Robinson, XPO Logistics (the brokerage piece), Hub Group. These businesses don't own trucks or planes; they match shipper demand to carrier capacity, earning a margin between the two. Working-capital intensive, technology-leveraged, less capital-heavy than parcel or LTL. (4) Contract logistics and warehousing — GXO, DSV, Kuehne + Nagel — runs warehouses and distribution centers on multi-year contracts with retailers and manufacturers. Buffett's broader Berkshire portfolio includes McLane (food-and-beverage distribution to convenience stores) which operates on the same density-and-scale economics. The 2020–2022 pandemic produced the most dramatic logistics cycle in modern history (rates spiked 4–8x in container shipping; LTL pricing peaked; air-freight rates spiked); the 2023–24 reset is the canonical recent normalization. Wisdom-tradition writers have engaged this industry less than most; Howard Marks's writing on cycles applies directly to the spot-rate-driven pieces.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The American Trucking Associations (ATA) for U.S. freight-industry data. Cass Freight Index for monthly U.S. freight shipments. Drewry and Freightos for international ocean and air-freight pricing. Damodaran's logistics data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on network-effect and density moats. Specific industry trade press — Journal of Commerce, FreightWaves, DC Velocity.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Air Freight & Logistics businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
