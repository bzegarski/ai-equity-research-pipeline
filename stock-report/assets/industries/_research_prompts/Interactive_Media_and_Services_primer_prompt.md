## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the INTERACTIVE MEDIA & SERVICES industry — Alphabet (Google), Meta, Pinterest, Snap, Reddit, Match Group, ZipRecruiter, plus Chinese internet platforms — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the platform-aggregation economics that produce some of the most profitable businesses in human history (Alphabet's Google Search, Meta's Facebook + Instagram + WhatsApp, Tencent's WeChat ecosystem), the network-effect moats that compound on scale, the regulatory pressure on Big Tech that frames the present strategic environment, and Stratechery's aggregation theory as the canonical contemporary primer.

What to attend to particularly for Interactive Media & Services. The industry's defining feature is platform-and-aggregation economics. The strongest businesses earn 25–35%+ operating margins, near-zero capital intensity per incremental user, and structural network-effect or coordination moats that are essentially impossible to replicate. (1) Search and the Google ecosystem — Alphabet (Google Search the canonical example, plus YouTube, Google Cloud, Other Bets including Waymo). Google Search has earned operating margins in the 30%+ range for over a decade with structural growth from search-volume increases plus query-monetization improvements. The 2024–25 generative-AI threat (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude as alternative search interfaces) is the single most important present strategic question. (2) Social media — Meta Platforms (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads, Reality Labs/VR), Pinterest, Snap, Reddit (IPO'd 2024). Meta's "family of apps" economics — operating margin recovery through 2023–24 after the 2022 efficiency reset, the Reality Labs ($16B+ annual losses on VR/AR investment) as the controversial reinvestment spend. (3) Specialty interactive — Match Group (Tinder, Hinge, Match.com), Bumble, Pinterest, Reddit, Trip.com (China travel), Booking Holdings (also Hotels). Each is its own niche with network-effect economics scaled to the specific category. (4) Chinese internet platforms — Tencent (WeChat, gaming, online ads — Hong Kong-listed), Alibaba (e-commerce plus cloud), JD.com, Pinduoduo, NetEase, Baidu (search, AI). Structurally similar economics to U.S. peers but exposed to China-specific regulatory and geopolitical risk. (5) Online classifieds and marketplaces — Match Group sits here partially; Zillow Group (real estate; classified Real Estate or Interactive Media depending on source); Yelp; ZipRecruiter; Indeed (within Recruit Holdings). The wisdom-tradition references for this industry beyond Munger's commentary are limited; the contemporary practitioner literature is essential. Stratechery by Ben Thompson on aggregation theory is the canonical reference — the Aggregator Era (2015) frame and the related "platform vs aggregator" distinction is foundational. Mauboussin's writing on intangible-asset compounders applies. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on network-effect moats with worked examples in interactive media is the closest "wisdom-tradition" treatment. Munger has been famously regretful about missing Google publicly (Daily Journal annual meetings, multiple years) — that admission alone is informative about how the wisdom tradition has updated its framework on these businesses. Buffett bought a small Alphabet position in late 2024 (per 13-F filings) — informative about the framework's continuing evolution.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Stratechery by Ben Thompson — aggregation theory and the platform-vs-aggregator distinction is the canonical primer. Munger's Daily Journal commentary on Google (cite year). Mauboussin's Counterpoint Global papers on intangible assets and growth durability. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on network-effect moats. eMarketer / Insider Intelligence for digital-ad-spend data where freely summarized. Damodaran's interactive-media data. Specific industry trade press — Digiday, Search Engine Land, AdExchanger.

Length: 4,000–5,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Interactive Media & Services businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
