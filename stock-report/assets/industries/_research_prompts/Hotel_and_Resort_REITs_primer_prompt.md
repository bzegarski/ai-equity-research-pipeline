## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the HOTEL & RESORT REITs industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for why hotel REITs are the most cyclically exposed corner of the REIT universe, the key operating metrics (RevPAR, ADR, occupancy), the structural difference between owning hotels (capital-heavy, operating-leverage exposure) and the brand-and-management-fee economics covered separately under Hotels Restaurants & Leisure, and why the 2020 pandemic produced one of the deepest sector-specific stress tests in REIT history.

What to attend to particularly for Hotel & Resort REITs. The industry's defining feature is real-estate ownership of hotels under brand-and-management arrangements with the major lodging operators (Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG). The REITs typically own the building and pay management fees plus brand-license fees to the operator; the operator runs the hotel under its brand. This separation of real-estate ownership from operating activity is the structural dynamic. The economic engine is the hotel's cash flow after operating expenses, management fees, brand fees, FF&E (furniture, fixtures, equipment) reserves, and capex. Hotel REITs are the most cyclically exposed corner of the REIT universe because hotel occupancy and rate move within months of demand changes, unlike multi-year leases at office or industrial REITs. The canonical names — Host Hotels & Resorts (the largest U.S. hotel REIT; high-end full-service portfolio; Marriott / Hyatt / Westin brands), Park Hotels & Resorts (the 2017 Hilton spin), Pebblebrook Hotel Trust (urban and resort focused), RLJ Lodging Trust, Apple Hospitality REIT (focused on select-service hotels — Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt limited-service brands), DiamondRock Hospitality, Sunstone Hotel Investors, Summit Hotel Properties, Service Properties Trust, Chatham Lodging Trust. International — Host's international peers are mostly held privately. The cycle exposure: the 2008–09 collapse saw hotel-REIT share prices decline 70–90% from peak; the 2020 pandemic produced an even deeper short-term stress (Pebblebrook's stock fell ~80%; Host fell ~70%) followed by an aggressive 2021–24 recovery. RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room = ADR × occupancy) is the canonical metric for hotel-industry pricing-and-demand health. STR Global is the industry data source. The wisdom-tradition references for this industry are extremely sparse — Howard Marks on cycles in capital-heavy operating-leverage businesses applies; the 2020 stress test produced significant practitioner writing in the immediate aftermath. The single most useful frame: hotel REITs are the cyclical-extreme corner of the REIT universe — closer to operating-leverage-exposed cyclicals than to bond-proxy REITs — and require analyzing through-the-cycle metrics rather than peak-cycle multiples.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. STR Global for hotel-industry RevPAR data (the canonical industry reference). NAREIT REIT 101 series. Howard Marks on cyclical-business cycles. Damodaran's hotel-REIT data. Specific industry trade press — Hotel News Now, Skift, Hotel Management.

Length: 3,000–4,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Hotel & Resort REITs in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
