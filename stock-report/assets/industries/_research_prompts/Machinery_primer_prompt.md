## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the MACHINERY industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the original-equipment-vs-aftermarket structure that defines machinery economics, the dealer network's role in moats, the cyclicality of equipment demand, and the historical pattern of how the best operators (Caterpillar, Deere, Cummins) compound through cycles by harvesting parts and service revenue.

What to attend to particularly for Machinery. The defining feature of machinery economics is the original-equipment-vs-aftermarket split. New equipment sales are deeply cyclical and follow customer capex (mining, construction, agriculture, oil and gas). Aftermarket — replacement parts, service contracts, attachments, used-equipment trade-ins — runs at much higher margins (typically 25–35% gross margin vs 15–25% on new equipment) and compounds steadily as the installed base grows. Caterpillar, Deere, Cummins, Parker Hannifin, Ingersoll Rand, Westinghouse Air Brake (Wabtec), AGCO, Oshkosh, Illinois Tool Works (ITW also classified as Industrial Conglomerates), Dover, Stanley Black & Decker — each of these names has a different mix between new equipment and aftermarket. The dealer network is the moat axis that distinguishes the strongest operators. Caterpillar's global dealer network — independently owned dealers in territories that the dealer typically holds for decades — produces the integrated-customer-experience moat that makes Caterpillar the cost-and-capability leader in heavy construction and mining equipment. Deere's North American agricultural dealer network operates similarly with farmers. Replacing a Caterpillar excavator on a job site means replacing the operator-training relationship, the parts-availability geography, and the financing relationship — not just the machine. Wisdom-tradition writers have engaged Machinery moderately: Buffett has written about ISCAR (cutting tools, 2006 acquisition; commentary across letters) and Marmon (2008 letter, multiple industrial holdings). Pat Dorsey's Little Book on switching-cost and dealer-network moats applies. The agricultural cycle differs from the construction cycle differs from the mining cycle differs from the oil-and-gas equipment cycle; the strongest machinery operators are diversified across these to smooth single-end-market cycles. Used-equipment values are an important leading indicator of pricing pressure — falling used-equipment prices typically precede falling new-equipment orders by 6–12 months.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's ISCAR, Marmon, and Precision Castparts commentary across Berkshire letters. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on switching-cost and dealer-network moats. The Association of Equipment Manufacturers (AEM) for U.S. industry data. The U.S. Census Manufacturers' Shipments data (free monthly) — particularly mining and construction machinery shipments. Damodaran's machinery data. Specific industry trade press — Diesel Progress, Construction Equipment, Farm Equipment magazine.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Machinery businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
