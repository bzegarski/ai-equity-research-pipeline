## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the SPECIALIZED REITs industry — data center REITs, cell tower REITs, self-storage REITs, gaming REITs, prison REITs, timber REITs, farmland REITs, and other specialty property types — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the structurally distinct economics of each major specialized-REIT sub-segment, why specialized REITs typically trade at higher multiples than diversified peers (more scarcity, more thematic-investing flow), and the AI-data-center demand wave (2023–25) reshaping data-center REIT economics in real time.

What to attend to particularly for Specialized REITs. The industry covers nearly a dozen distinct property-type REITs with very different economics. The primer should treat the major sub-segments separately. (1) Data center REITs — Equinix (the canonical interconnection / colocation leader, with operating margins driven by cross-connect economics that go beyond pure square-foot leasing), Digital Realty Trust (large hyperscale focus). The 2023–25 AI capex super-cycle has driven record demand growth for both, with hyperscale developers building near-record capacity. (2) Cell tower REITs — American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA Communications. (Covered in Wireless Telecommunication Services prompt; the REIT structure is the relevant frame here.) Long-cycle ground-lease economics, multi-tenant amenable. (3) Self-storage REITs — Public Storage (the largest), Extra Space Storage, CubeSmart, National Storage Affiliates Trust. Structurally healthy because storage demand is non-cyclical (driven by life-event triggers — divorce, death, downsizing, moving), and the industry has consolidated through institutional roll-up over decades. The 2024–25 self-storage market has experienced supply-driven moderation in some Sun Belt markets after the 2018–22 development boom. (4) Gaming REITs — VICI Properties (the canonical case; the 2017–18 spin from Caesars Entertainment with subsequent landmark MGM Properties acquisition; 100% triple-net leased to gaming operators), Gaming and Leisure Properties (PENN Entertainment spin). Long-cycle leases (typically 30+ year initial terms with renewals), state-licensed gaming-tenant lock-in, near-zero operational risk to the REIT — the structural framework is closer to corporate net-lease than to traditional REIT. (5) Prison REITs (Government Real Estate) — CoreCivic, GEO Group. Structurally controversial; operate prisons under government contracts; ESG-investor exclusion lists in many cases. (6) Timber REITs — Weyerhaeuser, Rayonier, PotlatchDeltic. (Also classified Materials / Paper & Forest Products in some screens.) Structurally attractive — biological timber growth at 3–7% per year regardless of macro; long-duration land asset; harvest-and-sell economics. (7) Farmland REITs — Gladstone Land Corporation, Farmland Partners. Smaller listed universe; structural growth from agricultural-asset values plus rent-yield. (8) Specialty cold storage — Lineage (IPO'd 2024), Americold Realty Trust. Structurally faster-growing than ambient industrial because of supply chain-resilience demand. (9) Iron and infrastructure — Iron Mountain (information storage and management; classified Specialized REIT but increasingly digital-services-focused), Lamar Advertising (also Communication Services-Media for outdoor advertising). The wisdom-tradition references for specialized REITs are sparse beyond NAREIT primer material — Howard Marks on cycle-positioning in long-duration cash-flow businesses applies; Pat Dorsey on regional / regulatory / network-effect moats applies to the strongest sub-segments (cell towers, data centers). The single most useful frame: specialized REITs trade at higher multiples than diversified peers because each sub-segment has its own scarcity story (cell towers are oligopoly; data centers benefit from AI capex super-cycle; gaming REITs are a small triple-net lock-in universe), and the analytical move is to ask whether each individual specialty has structural growth runway sufficient to justify its valuation premium.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. NAREIT REIT 101 series. SemiAnalysis on AI data-center demand and capex cycles. The Self Storage Association for U.S. self-storage data. The International Association of Gaming Advisors for gaming-REIT context. NAREIT specialty-segment reports. Damodaran's specialized-REIT data. Specific industry trade press — Data Center Frontier, FierceWireless (for towers), Inside Self-Storage.

Length: 4,000–5,500 words (Specialized REITs earns extra length because of the variety of sub-segments). Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Specialized REITs in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
