## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the PAPER & FOREST PRODUCTS industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for why timberland is the most attractive piece of this industry structurally, why lumber producers are deeply cyclical with housing starts, why paper itself is in long-running secular decline (and which specific paper sub-segments are not), and how the REIT structure changes the analysis for some of the timber-heavy operators.

What to attend to particularly for Paper & Forest Products. The industry is best understood as four very different sub-businesses. (1) Timberland — Weyerhaeuser, Rayonier, PotlatchDeltic — is the most attractive piece structurally because trees grow biologically at 3–7% per year regardless of macroeconomic conditions, the underlying land has long-duration value, and the major U.S. timber operators have converted to REIT structures to pass through earnings tax-efficiently. The investor question is the implied price per acre vs comparable transactions, plus the harvest-rate vs growth-rate (sustainable harvest preserves the asset; over-harvest depletes it). (2) Lumber and engineered wood — Weyerhaeuser's wood-products segment, West Fraser, Canfor, Boise Cascade, LP Building Solutions — is deeply cyclical with U.S. housing starts (the 2020 pandemic lumber spike to $1,700/thousand-board-feet and the 2023 reset to $400 is the canonical recent cycle). Capacity utilization, mill costs, and downtime decisions are the operational levers. (3) Pulp and paper — International Paper (corrugated), Suzano (BEKP), Domtar, Resolute Forest Products, Sappi — has a secularly declining piece (printing-and-writing paper, newsprint, copier paper, all in long decline) and structurally healthy pieces (corrugated containerboard, tissue, fluff pulp for hygiene products). The corrugated piece overlaps with Containers & Packaging and benefits from e-commerce; the tissue piece is consumer-stable. The single biggest data point: U.S. printing-and-writing paper consumption has fallen ~70% since 2000. (4) Building products distribution — Builders FirstSource, BlueLinx — is wholesale distribution of lumber and engineered wood, with route-density and inventory-turnover economics distinct from the producer side. Wisdom-tradition writers have engaged this industry less than most; Howard Marks's commodity-cycle memos apply directly to lumber and pulp. Long capex cycles (a new pulp mill costs $1B+ and runs for 30+ years) define the industry's return pattern over time.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Forest Resources Association and AF&PA for industry-level data. Random Lengths and Madison's Lumber Reporter for lumber pricing. RISI for global pulp and paper benchmarks where freely summarized. Howard Marks on commodity-cycle businesses. Damodaran's paper and forest products data. NAREIT primer material for the timber-REIT structure.

Length: 3,000–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Paper & Forest Products businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
