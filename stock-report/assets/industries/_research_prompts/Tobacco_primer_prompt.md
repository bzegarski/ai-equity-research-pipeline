## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the TOBACCO industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for why tobacco has been one of the highest-returning industries in U.S. equities over the last sixty years despite secular volume declines, the addiction-and-brand pricing power that produces 50–70% operating margins, the regulatory and litigation overlay (the 1998 Master Settlement Agreement; FDA flavoring rules; the next-generation product transition), and the moral position the wisdom-tradition writers have taken on the industry.

What to attend to particularly for Tobacco. The industry's defining feature is the combination of severe regulatory pressure with extreme product-level pricing power, producing consistent 50–70% operating margins despite multi-decade unit-volume declines. The canonical names are Philip Morris International (PMI; international combustible tobacco plus IQOS heat-not-burn), Altria (Marlboro and other brands in U.S. only, plus Juul investment that was largely written down), British American Tobacco (BAT; U.K.-listed; Camel, Newport, Pall Mall plus Vuse vape), Imperial Brands, Japan Tobacco. The economics: U.S. cigarette unit volume has declined ~3–5% annually since 2000; pricing has compounded at 5–8% annually; revenue per pack has roughly tripled in nominal terms; gross margins have expanded as fixed-cost manufacturing leverage has grown despite volume declines. The 1998 Master Settlement Agreement (MSA) between the major U.S. tobacco companies and 46 state attorneys general fundamentally restructured the industry — it imposed annual settlement payments (still ongoing) and constrained marketing in ways that ironically reinforced the dominant brands' market positions by limiting new entrant marketing. The next-generation product transition (heat-not-burn, vaping, oral nicotine pouches) is the central present strategic question — IQOS at PMI is the most successful next-gen product globally; Juul's collapse and Altria's $12.8B Juul stake write-down (2023) is the canonical cautionary tale on big-tobacco-meets-startup. The wisdom-tradition writers have engaged tobacco rarely from an investment standpoint. Buffett famously refused to buy tobacco companies despite acknowledging they would have been excellent investments — his 2003 Berkshire letter and various commentary explain the moral position. Munger has been similarly explicit. Howard Marks's commentary on tobacco-style cyclical-but-durable businesses applies. Pat Dorsey's Little Book treats tobacco as the canonical addiction-driven brand-and-pricing-power moat. The investor reads Tobacco knowing this is a wisdom-tradition exclusion case — the analytics are clean but the moral question is real and load-bearing.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's various commentary on tobacco as a moral exclusion (2003 Berkshire letter and onward). The CDC's Tobacco Use data. The U.S. Surgeon General's reports for context on health-and-regulatory trajectory. Tobacco Tactics (University of Bath; tobacco-industry monitoring). Damodaran's tobacco-sector data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on addiction-and-brand moats. Specific industry trade press — Tobacco Business, ECigIntelligence.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Tobacco businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
