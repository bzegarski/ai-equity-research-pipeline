## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the HEALTH CARE REITs industry — REITs owning senior housing, skilled nursing, medical office buildings, and life-sciences properties — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the demographic-tailwind story (aging population) that drives long-cycle demand for senior housing and skilled nursing, the structurally different economics of NNN-leased medical office buildings vs operator-tied senior housing, the regulatory / reimbursement-cycle exposure of skilled-nursing REITs, and why life-sciences office REITs have produced different results from generic office.

What to attend to particularly for Health Care REITs. The industry has four structurally distinct sub-segments. (1) Senior housing — Welltower (the largest by far; senior-housing operating-portfolio plus triple-net), Ventas (senior housing plus medical office), American Healthcare REIT, Brookdale Senior Living (operating company; partner to multiple REITs). The senior-housing operator-relationship structure (RIDEA — REIT Investment Diversification and Empowerment Act, allowing REITs to participate in operating profits beyond pure rent) gives REITs operating-leverage exposure but adds operator-execution risk. The 2020 pandemic produced significant occupancy declines and operator stress; the 2022–24 recovery has been uneven. (2) Skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) — Omega Healthcare Investors (the largest SNF REIT), CareTrust REIT, Sabra Health Care REIT. Reimbursement-cycle exposure to Medicare Part A and state Medicaid is the central regulatory question; the post-2020 labor-cost inflation and the 2024 minimum-staffing rule are the recent operational headwinds. (3) Medical office buildings (MOBs) — Healthpeak Properties (post-2024 MOB-and-life-sciences focus), Healthcare Realty Trust (the canonical pure-play MOB REIT), Physicians Realty Trust (acquired by Healthcare Realty 2024), Global Medical REIT. NNN-leased physician-tenanted office buildings; structurally healthier than generic office because medical practices are sticky tenants tied to hospital-system referrals and compliance investments. (4) Life sciences office — Alexandria Real Estate Equities (the canonical case; campuses in Boston Cambridge, San Francisco Bay Area, San Diego, Research Triangle leased to biotech and pharma), Healthpeak Properties' life-sciences piece. Structurally more like high-spec office than medical-office; significant cap-rate compression through 2010s reversed in 2022–24 as biotech funding tightened. The wisdom-tradition references for this industry are sparse — Howard Marks's writing on long-duration cash-flow cycles applies; Pat Dorsey on regulated and demographic-tailwind moats applies. The single most useful frame: separate the operator-tied (senior housing, skilled nursing) from the NNN-tenanted (medical office) and the specialty (life-sciences) sub-segments; they have very different cycle exposures, regulatory risks, and growth profiles.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. NAREIT REIT 101 series for the canonical primer. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) for skilled-nursing reimbursement context. The National Investment Center for Seniors Housing & Care (NIC) for senior-housing data. Damodaran's REIT data. Pat Dorsey on real-estate moats. Specific industry trade press — Senior Housing News, Skilled Nursing News, MedCity News.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Health Care REITs in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
