## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the DIVERSIFIED CONSUMER SERVICES industry — for-profit education, personal services, specialty consumer services — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the heterogeneous mix of businesses inside this GICS bucket — for-profit education (highly regulated, cyclical with the credit cycle), personal services (specialty positioning), funeral services (consolidation-led roll-ups), and specialty consumer services — and how to think about each.

What to attend to particularly for Diversified Consumer Services. This is a small, heterogeneous GICS industry, so the primer should treat its parts separately. (1) For-profit education — Adtalem Global Education, Strategic Education, Grand Canyon Education, Laureate Education (privatized), American Public Education. The industry was structurally re-shaped by the 2010–2016 federal regulatory crackdown on for-profit colleges (gainful-employment rules, the closing of Corinthian Colleges and ITT Tech), the 2016–2020 Trump-era loosening, and the 2021–2024 Biden-era re-tightening. Cohort default rates, regulatory compliance exposure, and Title IV federal student-loan dependence are the central risk variables. K–12 online education (Stride / K12 Inc.) is a sub-segment with different demand drivers. (2) Funeral services — Service Corporation International (SCI), Carriage Services, Park Lawn (Canada). Roll-up businesses on regional cluster economics; SCI's three-decade M&A history is a worked example of disciplined consolidation in a fragmented industry. Pre-need contract economics (customers paying years in advance) and trust-fund float are the financial characteristics that make this industry interesting beyond its surface dullness. (3) Personal services and specialty — Service Corp's funeral peers; H&R Block (tax preparation, formerly Diversified Financials but now sometimes here); Weight Watchers International; Jack in the Box's franchise-services unit; specialty children-services businesses. Each is its own niche. (4) Childcare and senior care — Bright Horizons Family Solutions, Brookdale Senior Living. Demographics-driven demand; regulatory complexity; labor-cost inflation pressures. The wisdom-tradition writers have engaged this industry rarely — Howard Marks on regulatory cycles applies to for-profit education; Pat Dorsey's framing on regional consolidation moats applies to funeral services. The H&R Block vs TurboTax 2003–2025 trajectory (well documented in the wisdom-tradition literature) is the canonical lesson on what happens to a service-and-relationship incumbent when a software-led player automates the workflow.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard for for-profit education data. The National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) for funeral-industry data. Damodaran's diversified-services data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on regional-consolidation moats. Specific industry trade press — Inside Higher Ed, Funeral Service Insider.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Diversified Consumer Services businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
