## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the HOUSEHOLD DURABLES industry — homebuilders, appliances, home furnishings, leisure products — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the violently cyclical economics of homebuilders, the structurally healthier consumer-appliances and home-furnishings businesses, what makes Berkshire's homebuilding holdings (Clayton Homes) attractive vs the typical listed homebuilder, and how to read each sub-segment.

What to attend to particularly for Household Durables. The industry has four structurally different sub-segments. (1) Homebuilders — D.R. Horton, Lennar, NVR, Toll Brothers, PulteGroup, KB Home, Meritage Homes. Violently cyclical with U.S. housing starts; the 2006–2009 collapse was one of the deepest sectoral declines in modern U.S. equities (peak-to-trough share-price declines of 70–90% for most listed homebuilders). NVR's land-option-only model (no land bank inventory) was the canonical exception that thrived through the cycle while peers wrote down land assets. Land bank size, gross margin trajectory, mortgage-lending captive economics, and capital-allocation discipline through the cycle are the central management questions. The 2024–25 affordability crisis (median home prices rose ~50% from 2019 to 2024 against household-income growth of ~25%) is the current cycle pressure. (2) Home furnishings and appliances — Whirlpool, Helen of Troy, Williams-Sonoma, RH (Restoration Hardware), Mohawk Industries (flooring), La-Z-Boy. Tied to home turnover and remodeling cycles; structurally less cyclical than homebuilders but still exposed. (3) Consumer electronics and recreational vehicles — Garmin, GoPro, Polaris Industries, Brunswick, Marine Products, Yeti Holdings, BRP. Discretionary purchase categories with brand and category-position differentiation; Yeti and Garmin are the canonical premium-brand cases. (4) Manufactured housing — Clayton Homes (private, owned by Berkshire), Skyline Champion. Manufactured housing produces structurally lower-cost residential dwellings (typically 30–50% cheaper per square foot than site-built); the industry has consolidated significantly and the manufactured-home REITs (Equity LifeStyle, Sun Communities) own land beneath the homes. Buffett's Clayton Homes (acquired 2003) and his commentary on manufactured housing across Berkshire letters is the wisdom-tradition reference. NVR's idiosyncratic capital-light model is a worked operating-excellence case for the conventional-homebuilder space. The strongest indicator of homebuilder cycle position: months of supply (existing-home inventory / sales rate) against affordability ratios.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) for homebuilding data and the Housing Market Index. The U.S. Census Bureau Housing Starts and New Home Sales data (free monthly). The Joint Center for Housing Studies (Harvard) for housing-cycle commentary. Buffett's Clayton Homes commentary across Berkshire letters. Damodaran's homebuilding and appliances data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on brand moats in consumer durables.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Household Durables businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
