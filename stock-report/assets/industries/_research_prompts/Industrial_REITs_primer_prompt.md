## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the INDUSTRIAL REITs industry — warehouse and logistics REITs — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the structural growth thesis that has made industrial REITs one of the strongest-performing real-estate sub-segments of the past decade — e-commerce-driven warehouse demand, last-mile logistics infill, supply-chain reconfiguration. I should also understand the metrics (rental rate growth, occupancy, mark-to-market on lease renewals), the geographic-portfolio differentiation (port markets vs interior distribution hubs), and the 2022–24 cyclical reset within the structural uptrend.

What to attend to particularly for Industrial REITs. The industry's defining feature has been the e-commerce-and-supply-chain-reconfiguration secular thesis playing out from roughly 2010 through 2024–25. E-commerce penetration moves from ~5% of retail in 2010 to ~17% by 2024 in the U.S., and each percentage-point of e-commerce share consumes meaningfully more warehouse square footage per dollar of GMV than physical retail does. The result has been the strongest decade of industrial-REIT outperformance in the sector's history. Prologis is the canonical case — the largest industrial REIT globally, ~$200B+ in market value at peak, with strategic positioning in the highest-demand global logistics markets (West Coast U.S. ports, key Mexico industrial corridors, Northern European hubs, Brazil distribution). The other major listed names — Rexford Industrial (Southern California urban-infill specialist), Terreno Realty (six-major-port-market focus), First Industrial Realty Trust, EastGroup Properties (Southeast and Sun Belt), Stag Industrial (single-tenant focus), LXP Industrial Trust (mixed; transitioning), Lineage Inc. (cold storage; IPO'd 2024). Specialty cold-storage — Lineage and Americold Realty Trust are the listed cold-storage REITs with structurally different cap-rate profiles. The metrics — same-store NOI growth (the strongest industrial REITs have run 5–8% over the past five years; mid-2020s was peak with 8–12% in the 2021–22 supply-shortage cycle), occupancy (industrial peaks consistently at 95–97%), mark-to-market on lease renewals (the key forward indicator: when leases signed five-to-seven years ago expire and re-sign at current market rents, the rate-bump can be 30–60%; the strongest industrial REITs in port markets have shown "embedded growth" of mid-double-digits this way through 2024). The 2022–24 reset — industrial REIT shares fell 30–50% from late-2021 peaks as long-rates rose and as some markets saw temporary supply-and-demand re-balancing post-pandemic; the structural thesis remained intact. The wisdom-tradition references for this industry are sparse — NAREIT REIT 101 covers the structure; Howard Marks's commentary on real-estate cycles applies. The single most useful frame: industrial REITs are the structurally healthiest property-type REIT segment of the past decade because of the e-commerce thesis, but the analytical work required is the geographic and tenant-mix specifics — port-market industrial vs interior-distribution-hub industrial vs cold-storage have very different characteristics.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. NAREIT REIT 101 series. CBRE Research and JLL Research industrial reports. The U.S. Census Bureau E-Commerce data. Damodaran's REIT data. Prologis investor presentations as canonical primer material on global industrial-REIT economics. Specific industry trade press — Multi-Housing News, GlobeSt, Industrial Distribution.

Length: 3,000–4,500 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Industrial REITs in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
