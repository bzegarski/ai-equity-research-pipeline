## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the INDEPENDENT POWER AND RENEWABLE ELECTRICITY PRODUCERS industry — merchant power generators, renewable-energy developers, yieldco vehicles — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the structural difference between regulated utility economics (covered separately under Electric Utilities) and merchant / contracted power generation (this industry), the long-cycle PPA (power purchase agreement) economics that drive renewable-energy developers, the AI-data-center power demand that's reshaping merchant power markets through 2024–25, and the cautionary history of yieldco vehicles (the 2015–16 SunEdison / TerraForm collapse).

What to attend to particularly for Independent Power and Renewable Electricity Producers. The industry's defining feature is power generation outside the regulated rate-base framework — merchant power exposure to wholesale electricity prices, contracted PPA revenue from utility-and-corporate offtake agreements, or hybrid mixes. (1) Merchant power and IPPs — Vistra Corp (the canonical AI-power-cycle compounder; share price 4–5x from 2022–24 driven by AI data-center power demand expectations), Constellation Energy (post-Exelon spin; nuclear and clean-power focus), NRG Energy, AES Corporation (international IPP), Calpine (private), Talen Energy (post-bankruptcy emergence), PSE&G (mixed). Merchant generators sell power into wholesale markets (PJM, ERCOT, MISO, ISO-NE, NYISO, CAISO, SPP) and capture spot prices subject to capacity-market and ancillary-services revenues. (2) Renewable-energy independent developers — NextEra Energy Partners (the yieldco vehicle of NextEra; structurally challenged 2023–24 with cost-of-capital pressure), Brookfield Renewable Partners, Clearway Energy, Ormat Technologies (geothermal), Sunnova Energy (residential solar), Sunrun (residential solar; subscription business model), Array Technologies (solar tracker manufacturer; not pure IPP but adjacent). (3) Independent-power yieldcos and renewable-asset listings — historically yieldcos (Pattern Energy, NRG Yield) raised capital cheaply by paying high distribution yields and acquiring assets from sponsors at attractive yields-on-acquisition; the 2015–16 SunEdison / TerraForm Power collapse exposed the fragility of the model when access to cheap capital evaporates. The post-2020 yieldco generation (Clearway, NextEra Energy Partners) has had to navigate cost-of-capital pressure with discipline; many have de-listed or been re-acquired by parents. (4) Dedicated solar / wind developers and operators — First Solar (manufacturer plus project developer), Enphase Energy (residential solar inverters; not IPP per se), Plug Power (hydrogen fuel cell — speculative end of the spectrum), Bloom Energy (fuel cells). (5) AI-data-center power — Constellation, Vistra, and Talen have all been re-rated dramatically through 2024–25 on AI data-center power demand; Microsoft's Three Mile Island restart deal (Constellation, 2024) and Amazon's Talen nuclear deal are the canonical worked examples. The wisdom-tradition references — Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway Energy includes substantial renewable-generation exposure (Iowa wind, multiple solar plants); the broader Berkshire commentary on long-cycle infrastructure investment applies. Howard Marks on cycles in capital-heavy infrastructure businesses applies. The single most useful frame: distinguish merchant exposure (high upside, cyclical risk, AI-power-demand-cycle leverage) from contracted / regulated exposure (utility-like cash flow profile) — the strongest IPPs have a mix; pure-merchant exposure is structurally riskier than yield-focused investors typically appreciate.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) for wholesale-power market regulatory framework. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) Electric Power Monthly. The American Clean Power Association for renewables industry data. The U.S. Department of Energy National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) for technology-cost trajectories. The 2015–16 SunEdison bankruptcy court filings as the canonical worked example of yieldco fragility. Damodaran's IPP-and-renewables data. Specific industry trade press — Greentech Media (now part of Wood Mackenzie), Utility Dive, S&P Global Commodity Insights summaries.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Independent Power and Renewable Electricity Producers in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
