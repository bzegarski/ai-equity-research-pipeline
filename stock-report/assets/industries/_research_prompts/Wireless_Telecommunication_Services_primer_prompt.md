## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the WIRELESS TELECOMMUNICATION SERVICES industry — pure-play wireless carriers and tower owners — to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the U.S. three-carrier oligopoly economics (T-Mobile, AT&T Mobility, Verizon Wireless), the spectrum-and-network capex super-cycle (4G 2010–14, 5G 2019–24, 6G 2027–28+), the prepaid and MVNO sub-segments, and the structurally compelling tower-owner business model (American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA Communications) that earns near-utility-style returns on long-cycle ground leases.

What to attend to particularly for Wireless Telecommunication Services. The industry has two structurally distinct sub-segments. (1) Wireless carriers — T-Mobile US, AT&T Mobility (within AT&T), Verizon Wireless (within Verizon Communications), U.S. Cellular, Dish Network's wireless build-out (struggling). The U.S. has consolidated to a three-carrier oligopoly (the 2020 T-Mobile / Sprint merger completed the consolidation), with structural pricing discipline that's earned the carriers improving margins through 2023–24. The economics — ARPU (average revenue per user; ~$50/month for postpaid), churn (typically 1–2% monthly for postpaid, 3–5% for prepaid), spectrum capex cycles, and the multi-year network-modernization cycles for 5G build-outs. T-Mobile's 2010s share-taking strategy (Un-Carrier campaigns, mid-band 5G leadership) is the canonical operating-excellence case. The fixed-wireless-access expansion of 2022–25 is the most interesting recent strategic move — T-Mobile and Verizon now offer home internet over their wireless networks, threatening cable broadband incumbents. (2) Cell tower owners — American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA Communications. REIT-structured (most in the U.S.), they own the physical towers and lease space to wireless carriers under long-duration contracts (typically 5–10 year initial terms with auto-renewals). The economics: tower acquisition or construction is capital-intensive once but produces ground-lease cash flow for decades; revenue per tower compounds as additional carriers, additional 5G technology, and small cells are added. American Tower is the canonical compounder with multi-decade returns above the industry. (3) Prepaid and MVNO — TracFone (acquired by Verizon 2021), Boost Mobile (within Dish), private prepaid players. Lower-margin, higher-churn sub-segment. The wisdom-tradition references include Buffett's brief 2020 Verizon position; Howard Marks on infrastructure cycles; Pat Dorsey's Little Book on regulatory-and-physical-asset moats with explicit tower-REIT treatment. The 2025–28 spectrum auction cycle (the FCC's 5.9 GHz, 12.7 GHz, and lower-3 GHz bands) is the present regulatory backdrop that will reshape capex needs. The 6G research-and-standardization timeline (3GPP Release 21 around 2027–28) is the next-cycle event horizon.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. Buffett's brief 2020 Verizon commentary. The CTIA (the Cellular Telecommunications Industry Association) for U.S. wireless industry data. The FCC for spectrum auction context. NAREIT primer material for the tower-REIT structure. Damodaran's wireless-sector data. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on tower-REIT moats and on regulated-physical-asset moats. Specific industry trade press — FierceWireless, Light Reading, RCR Wireless News.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Wireless Telecommunication Services businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
