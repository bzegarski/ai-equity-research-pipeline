## The prompt (copy-paste into Claude.ai's Research mode)

```
What does an individual long-term investor in the Buffett / Graham / Fisher / Munger / Marks tradition need to know about the HOTELS, RESTAURANTS & LEISURE industry to read a single-company writeup intelligently and to ask the right questions when studying any business in this industry?

Who I am. A long-term individual investor, not a professional analyst. The reading list shaping the writing I want is The Essays of Warren Buffett (Cunningham), Poor Charlie's Almanack, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom), The Investment Checklist (Shearn), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd), The Most Important Thing (Marks). These books teach by anchoring abstractions to concrete businesses.

What I want from a primer. After reading it, I should have intuition for the franchise vs owned model split that defines hotel and restaurant economics, the unit-economics framework (RevPAR for hotels, same-store sales for restaurants), the very different cycle exposure of cruise lines and casinos vs limited-service restaurants, and the brand-driven moats that make specific operators (McDonald's, Marriott, Hilton, Booking) consistently the best businesses in the sub-segment.

What to attend to particularly for Hotels, Restaurants & Leisure. Three structurally different business models drive the industry. (1) Hotels — Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG, Choice Hotels, Wyndham. The dominant operators have transitioned to asset-light franchise-and-management-fee models over the last three decades; they own few hotels and earn fees from owner-operators using their brands. RevPAR (Revenue per Available Room = ADR × occupancy) is the canonical metric. Pipeline (rooms under construction or signed) is the leading indicator of unit growth. The 2020 pandemic saw RevPAR drop 50%+ globally; the 2022–24 recovery overshoot is the canonical recent cycle. (2) Restaurants — McDonald's, Starbucks, Chipotle, Domino's, Yum Brands, Restaurant Brands International (Burger King, Tim Hortons, Popeyes), Darden (Olive Garden, LongHorn). Same-store sales growth, store-level four-wall margin, and franchisee returns are the key metrics. Limited-service / quick-service / fast-casual is structurally healthier than casual dining; chicken concepts (Chick-fil-A, Wingstop, Popeyes) have compounded faster than burger concepts in the recent decade. McDonald's is the canonical Buffett-quality franchise (commented on across multiple Berkshire letters). (3) Cruise lines — Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian. Capital-heavy, cyclically extreme, exposed to fuel costs, regulatory shifts, and pandemic risk; the 2020 collapse and the 2024 capacity-discipline recovery is the recent analog. (4) Casinos and gaming — MGM Resorts, Wynn Resorts, Las Vegas Sands, DraftKings (online). Structurally exposed to consumer credit cycles and to regulatory expansion of gaming in new jurisdictions. (5) Leisure travel facilitators — Booking Holdings, Airbnb, Expedia, TripAdvisor. Network-effect and aggregation-theory businesses; Booking is the canonical case study, with operating margins above 30% and structural advantages in supply (hotel inventory) liquidity. Wisdom-tradition writers have engaged this industry richly — Buffett's See's Candies (technically Discretionary too) teaches the brand-moat lessons that apply to McDonald's; Buffett's commentary on consumer franchise value in the 1988 Coca-Cola letter and elsewhere applies; Pat Dorsey's Little Book on brand and switching-cost moats applies; Stratechery's aggregation theory framing is the canonical contemporary primer for Booking and the OTA stack.

Style I love. Concrete subjects acting on concrete objects. One specific testable example per abstract claim. Well-chosen historical analogs. Plain English ahead of jargon, jargon defined inline on first use. Sentence rhythm. The small earned surprise.

Style I reject. Institutional sell-side templates (target prices, CAPM, beta-driven discount rates). Generic macro framing. Formulaic SWOT and pasted Porter five-forces. Numbers without narrative. Throat-clearing prose.

Source preferences. STR Global for hotel-industry data (the canonical RevPAR source). The National Restaurant Association for restaurant-industry data. Buffett's commentary on consumer franchises across Berkshire letters. Stratechery on aggregation theory and OTA economics. Pat Dorsey's Little Book on consumer-brand moats. Damodaran's hotel and restaurant data. Specific industry trade press — Hotel News Now, Skift, Nation's Restaurant News.

Length: 3,500–5,000 words. Organize however best serves the answer.

Note: this is research about how to think about Hotels, Restaurants & Leisure businesses in general. Don't analyze any specific company as an investment.
```
