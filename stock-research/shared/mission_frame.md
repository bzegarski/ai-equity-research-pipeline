FRAMING RULES FOR THIS STOCK RESEARCH

I am researching a specific public company using the most rigorous,
transferable parts of elite long-term investor process.

I am NOT asking for: a generic stock summary, a quick bull/bear case,
price-target recommendations, or quantitative ratio tables.

I AM asking:
- How does this business actually work at the unit level?
- What are its real competitive advantages, with evidence?
- Is the competitive position strengthening or weakening?
- What could destroy this business in 10 years?
- Are the managers good stewards of capital?
- Is the accounting honest?
- What would have to be true for this stock to be attractive?
- How could this thesis fail?

RULES:
1. Use only primary sources: SEC filings, annual reports, proxy statements,
   transcripts, competitor filings, industry/government data.
2. Do not borrow management's framing unless verifying it against evidence.
3. Separate evidence from interpretation from speculation. Label each.
4. Preserve uncertainty. "I don't know" and "the evidence is ambiguous"
   are valid conclusions.
5. Ground every claim in a specific source. If you cannot cite a source,
   label the claim as interpretation.
6. When assessing competitive advantages or disruption risks, provide
   concrete mechanisms and historical precedents — not generic labels.
